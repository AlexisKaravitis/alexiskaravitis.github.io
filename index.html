Fatigue-Testing Machine for Elastomer Life Characterization

A purpose-built rig for measuring the fatigue life of elastomers under cyclic loading, designed end to end from dynamics to manufacturable hardware.

// Overview

Designing a fatigue testing rig for elastomer specimens, from dynamic model through to a sized, manufacturable design. It combines Simulink dynamic modelling, CATIA V5 CAD and FEA-based component sizing in one workflow. Elastomers govern ride, NVH and durability across tyres, bushings, mounts and dampers, and they’re hard to model, which makes their fatigue life one of the trickier problems in vehicle development.

// Method
  1. Modelled the filled rubber as a nonlinear spring + damper (F = k₁d + k₃d³) capturing stiffness and loss
  2. Derived exact slider-crank kinematics, then per-specimen forces ×12 with drivetrain inertia
  3. Sized motor power and flywheel inertia to hold speed within the ripple limit
  4. Optimised over six solvers plus a 4000-sample random search on a single weighted cost
  5. Verified the dynamics with a hand ODE, a Simulink diagram and a self-deriving Simscape network
// Highlights
  • Slider-crank rig fatigue-tests 12 rubber specimens at 5 Hz, 57 mm stroke (ISO 132)
  • Sized drivetrain: 2.2 kW motor and a 98 kg flywheel, optimised down from an infeasible 384 kg
  • 6 optimisation solvers converge on one optimum; a 4000-sample search confirms within 0.4%
  • Cross-validated three ways — hand ODE, Simulink, Simscape — agreeing to ~3 ppm (MATLAB + Octave)
  • Hands off 5.1 kN / 1.27 kN peak loads to Abaqus FEA; ~4.6 days to a 2M-cycle test at 5 Hz

Constitutive Material Parameters in AM Structures using AI

An FEA and machine-learning workflow for predicting the properties of 3D-printed laminates.

// Overview

An AI-optimisation workflow that predicts 3D-printed material behaviour and cuts physical testing. Built an LSTM model in MATLAB and used Genetic Algorithms to optimise the FEA, so machine learning, simulation and material science ran as one pipeline. Modelled FDM layers as orthotropic plies under classical laminate theory to capture the anisotropy, then predicted stress–strain response. The workflow replaced much of the physical testing for a hard-to-model material class.

// Method
  1. Modelled FDM-printed layers as orthotropic plies via classical laminate theory
  2. Ran a GA-driven inverse FEA in Abaqus to identify six elastic constants per specimen
  3. Trained neural networks to predict each stiffness from the print parameters
  4. Validated with leave-one-out cross-validation and bagging across 24 specimens
  5. Combined machine learning, FEA and material science into one identification pipeline
// Highlights
  • Identifies 6 orthotropic elastic constants (E₁,E₂,ν,G₁₂,G₁₃,G₂₃) of FDM-printed PLA from print parameters
  • GA-driven inverse FEA in Abaqus: mean identification error 0.0022 (flat), 0.0066 (on-edge)
  • Neural network reaches R² ≈ 0.78 and ~8–9% relative RMSE on the primary stiffnesses
  • Trained on 24 PLA tensile specimens (ASTM D3039) with leave-one-out cross-validation + bagging
  • GA runs ~172 Abaqus evaluations in ~70 s per specimen (population 30, ≤60 generations)

FEA of a Formula Student Differential Mount

Abaqus linear-static and modal analysis of a CNC-machined drivetrain mount from a Formula Student car, with a four-material selection study.

// Summary

A finite-element study of a differential mount from a Formula Student car (Poseidon Racing), analysing how it transmits drivetrain reaction loads into the chassis. A mesh-converged C3D10 tetrahedral model was solved under the maximum bearing load, then a modal analysis and a four-material trade study (steel, Ti-6Al-4V, Al1050, Al7075-T6) were compared on strength, stiffness, mass and cost via a multi-criteria decision matrix.

// Method
  1. Simplified the CAD in SolidWorks and healed the geometry in SpaceClaim for a clean mesh
  2. Applied realistic BCs — fixed bolt holes plus a translational coupling at the bearing bore
  3. Loaded the bearing seat with the 4.5 kN maximum drivetrain reaction
  4. Ran a 19-mesh convergence study, then adopted a locally-refined C3D10 tetra mesh
  5. Solved static stress for four materials plus a Lanczos modal step; ranked with a decision matrix
// Key results
  • Peak von Mises stress 59.9 MPa under a 4.5 kN bearing load
  • Safety factor 8.4 (Al-7075-T6) vs 15.0 Ti, 4.2 steel, 0.5 Al1050 (fails)
  • Mass trimmed to 0.38 kg — best strength-to-weight-to-cost balance
  • Mesh-converged over 19 meshes (11k → 710k elements), <1% change
  • First six natural frequencies 15–219 Hz via a Lanczos modal step

// Method

Aerofoil CFD vs XFOIL + Offshore Wave Loading

Two-part study: RANS aerofoil aerodynamics validated against XFOIL, plus Morison-equation wave loading on a 7.5 m offshore monopile.

// Summary

A two-part fluid-mechanics study combining external aerodynamics with offshore structural loading. Part one compares XFOIL predictions against 2D RANS CFD in ANSYS Fluent for two NACA aerofoils and a bluff body at Re ≈ 53,300, on a boundary-layer-resolved mesh. Part two applies Airy linear-wave theory and the Morison equation to size the horizontal wave force on a 7.5 m-diameter monopile in 160 m of water.

// Method
  1. Computed Re and characterised the transitional boundary-layer regime
  2. Predicted CL, CD and Cm polars in XFOIL as the analytical baseline
  3. Built a C-domain, benchmarked three meshes, chose a y+<1 boundary-layer mesh
  4. Ran 2D RANS in Fluent, imposing AoA via inlet velocity components
  5. Overlaid XFOIL vs CFD polars, then sized the monopile force via depth-integrated Morison inertia
// Key results
  • 2D RANS on NACA 2421/0019 and a bluff body at Re ≈ 53,300
  • Boundary-layer mesh at y+ < 1 (10 layers); residuals < 1×10⁻⁶
  • Stall captured near 10° AoA; bluff-body CD ≈ 0.65–0.68
  • Wave case: wavelength 351 m, KC 1.26 → inertia-dominated regime
  • Peak horizontal wave force 1,260 kN (inertia:drag ratio 19.4)

Corrosion & Failure Analysis of Marine Steel

Electrochemical corrosion testing of mild steel in simulated seawater under agitation, cross-checked with mass-loss, tensile and SEM/EDS characterisation.

// Summary

A study of how solution agitation — a lab analogue of seawater current — accelerates corrosion of mild steel in 3.5% NaCl, mirroring the environment around offshore monopiles. Corrosion kinetics were tracked over six days via Open Circuit Potential and Linear Polarisation Resistance on a three-electrode potentiostat, then correlated against gravimetric mass loss, tensile testing and SEM/EDS, closing with a structured risk assessment.

// Method
  1. Immersed dog-bone specimens in 3.5% NaCl under four agitation conditions
  2. Ran daily OCP and LPR on a three-electrode PalmSens cell (Days 0–6)
  3. Converted polarisation resistance to corrosion rate (Stern-Geary, Faraday)
  4. Cross-checked with gravimetric mass loss and Instron tensile tests
  5. Characterised corrosion morphology with SEM/EDS and built a 5×5 risk matrix
// Key results
  • Corrosion rate roughly doubled stagnant → agitated: 0.09 → 0.26 mm/yr
  • Gravimetric mass loss scaled with flow: −0.06% → −0.34%
  • Kinetics tracked over a 6-day immersion via OCP + LPR
  • Rates via Stern-Geary + Faraday conversion (ASTM G59/G102)
  • Top failure risks scored 20/25 on a 5×5 likelihood–consequence matrix
// Tools & methods

ML Electricity-Demand Forecaster

A deterministic seasonal baseline corrected by per-horizon LightGBM residual regression to forecast the next 10 hours of UK grid demand.

// Summary

A hybrid electricity-demand forecaster predicting the next 10 hours of UK half-hourly grid load using a seasonal baseline corrected by per-horizon LightGBM residual regression. Trained on ~86,000 half-hourly records over five years with engineered calendar, cyclical, lag and rolling features. Twenty independent horizon models give a direct multi-step forecast, avoiding recursive error accumulation — a data-engineering discipline transferable to race-strategy and energy modelling.

// Method
  1. Engineered calendar, cyclical (sin/cos), lag and rolling-statistic features
  2. Built a deterministic seasonal baseline from prior-day and prior-week analogues
  3. Trained 20 per-horizon LightGBM residual models with recency-weighted sampling
  4. Controlled complexity with per-horizon early stopping, then refit on all data
  5. Blended baseline + residual via learned per-horizon scaling; evaluated on comparable days
// Key results
  • Validation RMSE 19.62 across a 20-point (10-hour) horizon
  • Trained on ~86,000 half-hourly points over five years
  • 20 per-horizon models → direct multi-step forecast, no error accumulation
  • Seasonal blend: yesterday 34.8% / last week 32.1% / two weeks 33.1%
  • Adaptive per-horizon confidence exposes where ML beats seasonality
