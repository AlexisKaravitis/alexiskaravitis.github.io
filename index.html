§06 PROJECTS & RESEARCH SELECTED · 2024 → 2026
// MSC THESIS · CRANFIELD · IN PROGRESS

Design of a Fatigue Testing Machine for Elastomer Life Characterization

A purpose-built rig for characterising the fatigue life of elastomers under cyclic loading — designed end-to-end from dynamics to manufacturable hardware.

Designing a fatigue testing rig for elastomer specimens, with the deliverable being a fully sized, manufacturable mechanical design. The work combines three disciplines into a single coherent workflow: Simulink dynamic modelling of the rig's loading behaviour, CATIA V5 CAD design of the mechanical structure, and FEA-based component sizing to validate stiffness, stress, and fatigue margins on the load-bearing parts.

Elastomers are the unglamorous workhorses of vehicle engineering — they live in tyres, suspension bushings, engine and gearbox mounts, dampers, and seals. Their fatigue behaviour governs ride quality, NVH, and durability, and characterising it well is one of the harder problems in automotive and motorsport R&D because rubber-like materials are highly non-linear, rate-dependent, and notoriously hard to model.

The thesis sits at the intersection of mechanical design, dynamics, structural simulation, and test engineering — the four disciplines that any serious development programme has to pull together to validate a new part against the road or the racetrack.

Simulink CATIA V5 FEA Dynamic Modelling Fatigue Analysis Elastomers Test Rig Design

Digital Twin & Demonstration of Autonomous Surface Vessels

A cooperative ASV fleet for unpowered tanker escort, with a full digital twin built to visualise live and historical telemetry.

AWARD · MAY 2026 Best Group Project Presentation Award — AME programme, Cranfield University.

Multidisciplinary group project building a cooperative fleet of autonomous tugboats for unpowered tanker escort scenarios. The system spanned Arduino-based vehicle hardware (Pololu motor drivers, LiPo power), MATLAB-based fleet control with Pure Pursuit guidance, AprilTag stereo camera localisation, and HTTP/WiFi command-and-control links.

Built a MATLAB 3D digital twin with extruded hull geometry, animated water surface, live telemetry dashboard and dual-CSV synchronisation — runnable both as standalone historical playback and as a live visualiser integrated with the tracking pipeline. Diagnosed WiFi latency as the dominant control bottleneck and rebuilt the comms layer around an async Python HTTP server with command queuing.

The presentation that won the award covered the digital twin alongside the live on-water demonstration, framing the work as a scalable approach to multi-agent autonomous vehicle development with low-cost hardware and high-fidelity simulation.

// BENG THESIS · UWA

Identification of Constitutive Material Parameters in AM Structures using AI

Combining FEA, machine learning and material science into a single optimisation workflow for FDM laminates.

Built an LSTM model in MATLAB for material behaviour prediction in Fused Deposition Modelling (FDM). Implemented Genetic Algorithms to optimise Finite Element Analysis simulations and produced an integrated AI-optimisation workflow that ties FEA, machine learning, and material science into a single pipeline.

Treated FDM-printed layers as orthotropic plies under classical laminate theory to model the inherent anisotropy of printed structures, then applied FEA incorporating laminate theory principles to predict stress–strain responses and optimise structural performance.

The result: a workflow that materially reduces reliance on physical testing while improving confidence in mechanical predictions for a notoriously hard-to-model class of materials.
